0 2 · The Product·Brava · Cedar Shake · Natural Cedar

Cedar, without the cedar.

Moulded from real shake, cast in polymer, approved for Florida wind.

Brava Cedar Shake is a composite tile moulded from moulds taken off real split cedar, then cast in polymer with the colour running all the way through rather than sprayed on the surface. Every shake carries the grain, the split lines and the irregular butt of the piece it was cast from, so the roof reads as a hand-split cedar roof from the street and from the drone shot.

What it is not is wood. A real cedar shake roof in Pinellas County spends its life in salt air, afternoon rain and ninety percent humidity, and it responds by cupping, splitting and growing things. It also carries an insurance conversation that most Florida carriers would rather not have. This product looks like the roof you want and behaves like a synthetic tile, which is why it holds Florida Product Approval #FL 41880.1 and a real cedar shake does not.

The profile specified is multi-width: 5″, 7″ and 12″ units mixed across each course. That mix is doing aesthetic work. A single-width shake lays up in a visible repeat, and on a house with roof planes this long the eye finds the pattern immediately. Three widths, staggered course to course, break the rhythm the way a hand-split roof does because a hand-split roof never had one width to begin with.

Because the colour is integral to the material rather than a coating, the failure mode of a painted or coated product does not apply here. There is no film to chalk off, and a chipped corner is the same Natural Cedar the rest of the shake is.

0 3 · The Scope·Brava Roofing System · Cedar Shake Profile

Exactly what we install.

Every line below is the contract wording. Nothing here is an allowance.

This is the full scope of work, as it appears in your contract.

  1. 0 1

    Contractor shall install Brava Cedar Shake (Synthetic/Polymeric) on the new construction residence located in Florida. Installation must comply with Florida Product Approval #FL 41880.1. Colour: Natural Cedar.

    The Florida Product Approval number is the state’s own file on this exact product and this exact assembly. It is what the Pinellas County inspector checks the installation against, and it is what an insurance carrier asks for later. Naming it in the contract means the installation method is not our opinion, it is a document.

  2. 0 2

    Product: Brava Cedar Shake (multi-width 5″, 7″, and 12″ units).

    Three widths, mixed course by course. On roof planes this long a single width would lay up as a visible repeating grid, and the whole point of a shake roof is that it does not look manufactured.

  3. 0 3

    Underlayment: Installation of 2 layers of High-Temperature Peel-and-Stick Underlayment (ASTM D1970) over the entire deck, as required for synthetic tiles in high-wind zones.

    This is the layer that actually keeps water out. The shake is the wear surface above it. Two fully adhered layers over the whole deck means the house is watertight before a single tile is set, and because peel-and-stick self-seals around every fastener, thousands of penetrations do not become thousands of small holes. High-temperature grade matters in Florida: a dark deck in August cooks standard membrane until it slumps and slides.

  4. 0 4

    Expansion Gaps: Shakes must be installed with a 3/8″ gap between units to allow for thermal expansion/contraction.

    Polymer moves with temperature, and a roof surface here swings enormously between a January night and an August afternoon. The gap gives every shake room to grow and shrink on its own. Butt them tight and a course has nowhere to go but up, which is how a synthetic roof ends up looking buckled two summers in.

  5. 0 5

    Exposure: Shakes shall be installed at a 10″ maximum weather exposure.

    Exposure is how much of each shake is left showing to the weather. Capping it at 10″ guarantees at least two thicknesses of material over every point on the roof. Stretching exposure is the standard way to make a tile roof cheaper, and it is invisible from the ground until the day it matters.

  6. 0 6

    Offset: A minimum 1.5″ side-lap (offset) must be maintained between joints in adjacent courses.

    Joints in neighbouring courses must never stack. A joint that lines up with the one below it is a channel pointing straight at the underlayment, and wind-driven rain finds it every time.

  7. 0 7

    Upper TPO roof installed per manufacturer specifications. Included in price.

    The upper section is low slope, and shake sheds water rather than sealing it, so tile up there would be the wrong material regardless of price. TPO is a single-ply membrane with heat-welded seams, which fuses the sheets into one surface instead of relying on laps and adhesive. Your contract puts it inside the roof price rather than pricing it separately. We still need the membrane brand, the thickness and the warranty term in writing before this page can describe it properly.

  8. 0 8

    Fastening: Per FBC Section 1507.15, fasteners shall be stainless steel (if within 3,000 ft of salt water) or hot-dipped galvanized #10 screws or 0.120″ ring-shank nails.

    The code makes the fastener depend on how close the house sits to salt water, and Dunedin sits on St. Joseph Sound. Which side of that line this address falls on is worth settling before the order goes in, because a fastener that corrodes is a roof held down by nothing while the tiles above it are still perfect. Tell us the answer and we will state the fastener outright instead of quoting the code’s either/or.

  9. 0 9

    Penetration: Fasteners must penetrate the 15/32″ plywood deck by at least 1/4″ or be fully embedded in 1″ thick wood planking.

    Uplift resistance lives in the grip of the shank in the wood, not in the head on top. A fastener that stops short of full penetration holds a fraction of its rated value, and the roof is only as good as its weakest thousand fasteners.

  10. 1 0

    HVHZ Compliance: If located in Miami-Dade/Broward, installation must follow the Miami-Dade NOA for Brava (High-Velocity Hurricane Zone) with specific screw patterns for perimeter and corner zones.

    This clause is statewide boilerplate and it is reproduced here because it is in your contract. This property is in Pinellas County, outside the High-Velocity Hurricane Zone, so the governing document is FBC 1507.15 and the Florida Product Approval, not the Miami-Dade NOA. Worth confirming so nobody reads it later as a spec that was skipped.

  11. 1 1

    Valleys: Contractor shall install Brava valleys over a layer of fully adhered underlayment per manufacturer specifications.

    A valley carries the runoff of two roof planes through one channel, so it moves more water faster than anywhere else on the house. It gets the manufacturer’s own valley component rather than a generic metal pan, over its own fully adhered layer underneath, which means the busiest part of the roof has two independent defences instead of one.

  12. 1 2

    Aluminium – Bronze Edge Metal: Drip edge must be installed per FBC 1503.2.1, fastened at 4″ on centre at the laps.

    Drip edge does two jobs. It throws water clear of the fascia so the trim on a brand new house does not start rotting from behind, and it closes the gap that wind would otherwise use to get under the first course. The 4″ on centre at the laps is tighter fastening than the field, because the laps are where the edge would peel first. The bronze finish is chosen to sit with Natural Cedar rather than cut a bright line across it.

  13. ✓

    Warranty

    Upon completion and full payment, Contractor will provide the homeowner with the Brava Limited Lifetime Warranty and the Contractor’s Workmanship Warranty for a period of 20 years.

Your contract carries no “contract includes” clause, so permits, dump fees, workers’ compensation, general liability and taxes are not addressed anywhere in it. We have deliberately left that as [TO BE CONFIRMED] rather than claim it.

0 4 · Optional·Upgrade to all Copper Metal

Copper at every edge.

Adds $15,000.00 to the contract if you initial it. It prints at $0.00 below because it is not selected on the estimate as signed.

This swaps the bronze aluminium drip edge for copper, and puts copper in the flashings wherever the detail calls for metal. Nothing else about the roof changes.

The case for it is that edge metal and flashings are simultaneously the most exposed parts of a roof and the most visible. On the aluminium specified in the base contract, the bronze is a finish over the metal. Copper is not finished at all. It is the same material at the surface as it is in the middle, so there is no coating to chalk, fade or wear off at the edges where the weather works hardest, and no bright base metal waiting underneath a scratch.

The contract puts it plainly: naturally corrosion resistant, no fading or chalking, and a patina that grows more attractive as it ages rather than less. On a coastal Pinellas house, corrosion resistance at the edge details is not a decorative argument.

The honest limitation, so you are not surprised: the patina is not something anybody controls. Copper leaves the truck bright, dulls within months, and takes years to move toward the darker tones and eventually the green most people picture. During that time it will not match itself perfectly from one elevation to another depending on sun and rain exposure, and copper runoff can stain masonry or stucco directly beneath it. If a uniform, predictable edge colour on day one matters more than the material, the bronze aluminium in the base contract is the better answer and there is no shame in it.

0 5 · Optional·Upgrade to Custom Colour

New Cedar, instead of Natural.

Adds $10,000.00 to the contract if you initial it. It prints at $0.00 below because it is not selected on the estimate as signed.

Same product, same profile, same installation. The only change is the colour of the shake: Brava Cedar Shake in New Cedar rather than the Natural Cedar named in the base contract.

Natural Cedar is the weathered tone, the colour a real shake roof arrives at after a few Florida summers. New Cedar is the other end of that life, the warmer, redder tone of cedar freshly split. Neither is a downgrade of the other, and because the colour is integral to the polymer rather than sprayed on, whichever you choose is the colour the roof stays.

The premium is not paint. It is a custom production run rather than the standard blend, which is where the $10,000.00 goes.

This is worth deciding early and deliberately. Almost everything else on a house can be revisited later. A roof colour on a home this size cannot be, and it is the single largest surface anyone will see from the street.
